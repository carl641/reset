What it is

At launch, the plunge is part of Contrast Therapy and is not booked on its own. What follows is what the cold half of that session involves. Cold plunging has a reputation for machismo that we have no interest in. The useful part is not how long you last. It is the moment your body wants to panic and you practice staying with it.

Sessions are short by design. Two to three minutes of exposure is where the research sits for most of the outcomes people come here for, and we would rather you do that consistently than do six minutes once and never return.

What it supports

  • Practices nervous system regulation under real stress
  • May reduce perceived muscle soreness after training
  • A reliable lift in alertness and mood for hours afterwards
  • Builds genuine stress tolerance over weeks, not minutes

Commonly booked for

  • Stress resilience
  • Morning energy and focus
  • Athletic recovery
  • Anyone who has been curious but nervous about cold

The Session

What the session is like.

  1. Before you get in

    We walk through the breathing first, on dry land. The exhale is longer than the inhale. That is most of the technique.

  2. In the water

    Two to three minutes for most people, less on your first visit. A timer you can see and someone in the room with you.

  3. The rewarm

    We let you rewarm naturally rather than jumping straight back into heat, unless you are doing a full contrast cycle.
